What this is for

You grew a culture, purified your protein, and read its absorbance at 280 nm. This works out how concentrated it is and how much your culture actually produced — then tells you whether that is a normal number for E. coli.

How much purified protein you ended up with, and how big the culture was. That gives yield in mg per litre.

How every number here is worked out

Concentration from A280

Beer–Lambert. The extinction coefficient can be given three ways, and they are not interchangeable:

A 0.1% — absorbance of a 1 mg/mL (1 g/L) solution in a 1 cm cuvette. This is what Benchling and ProtParam report, and the default here. c (mg/mL) = A / A0.1%. No MW needed.

E 1% — absorbance of a 10 mg/mL (1% w/v) solution. Exactly ten times A 0.1%, so mixing them up gives a 10-fold error. c (mg/mL) = 10 × A / E1%.

Molar ε (M⁻¹cm⁻¹) — c (M) = A / ε, then c (mg/mL) = c (M) × MW. MW required.

Replicate readings are averaged (SD shown for n ≥ 2). Dilution factor multiplies the result. Path length: NanoDrop's Protein A280 mode already reports a 10 mm-equivalent number, so no correction is applied by default.

Yield

Two steps. total (mg) = concentration (mg/mL) × volume of your prep (mL). Then yield (mg/L) = total (mg) ÷ culture volume (L), assuming the whole culture went into that one purification. Eluting into a bigger volume does not change how much protein you have — it is the same material spread thinner, so multiplying back out gives the same total.

Benchmarks for recombinant protein in E. coli:

< 10 mg/L — low
10–100 mg/L — typical shake flask
100–500 mg/L — high, optimised shake flask
0.5–10 g/L — very high, fed-batch bioreactor
> 10 g/L — at or beyond the best published titres
> 100 g/L — not physically possible

That ceiling is real: even a fed-batch reaching >100 g dry cell weight per litre only holds about 55 g/L of total cellular protein, since E. coli is roughly 52–55% protein by dry weight. More than that of a single product means a unit or volume mistake.

Standard curve

Ordinary least squares through your standards: A = slope × C + intercept, inverted as C = (A − intercept) / slope. R² below 0.99 is flagged, and a sample landing outside the concentration range you actually tested is flagged as an extrapolation.
