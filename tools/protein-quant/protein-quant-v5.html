Protein Quant

How much protein is in your tube, and how much your culture made.

You grew a culture, purified your protein, and read its absorbance at 280 nm. This works out how concentrated it is and how much your culture actually produced, then gives a literature-based sanity check for the expression host you pick.

STEP 1
Describe the protein
Its molecular weight and extinction coefficient. Both come from the sequence, e.g. Benchling or ProtParam.
STEP 2
Enter your A280 reading
Type all your replicate readings; they get averaged for you. You get concentration straight away.
STEP 3
Final volume + starting culture
The volume of your final purified sample (what you measured A280 on) and how big the culture was. Anything in between — resuspension, washes, dialysis — doesn't matter.
Not sure which number goes where? Walk through an example

Say this is your actual bench day: you grow a 200 mL overnight culture. You spin it all down and resuspend the pellet in 10 mL of lysis buffer, run it over a column, wash, and elute your protein in 500 µL. You read that 500 µL on the spectrophotometer.

What happened
Goes in the calculator as…
200 mL culture, grown overnight
Starting culture volume = 200 mL
Resuspended pellet in 10 mL lysis buffer
Not entered anywhere — this step changes concentration, not total protein, so it drops out of the maths entirely.
Column wash, dialysis, concentrating spin columns, etc.
Same as above — skip it, whatever you did.
Eluted protein, final volume 500 µL
Final purified volume = 500 µL
A280 read on that 500 µL
Goes straight into the A280 field in Step 2

With those numbers: concentration comes from your A280 reading as usual; total protein is that concentration × 0.5 mL; yield is that total ÷ 0.2 L culture. The 10 mL resuspension volume never appears in the calculation — only the volume of the tube you actually measured, and the culture size you grew it from.

Rule of thumb: if a step only moved your protein into a different buffer without adding or removing any of it, its volume does not matter. Only the very last volume — the one you measured — and the very first one — the culture — matter.

Standards

Read a dilution series of a known protein (usually BSA) with your colour reagent, then enter each concentration next to the absorbance you measured. A line (or a gentle curve, if you choose the quadratic fit) is fitted through them, and your unknowns are read off it.

Subtracting the blank removes the reagent's own colour from every standard and sample, so the line starts at the origin. Bradford responses flatten at high protein; if the top standards bend, the quadratic fit follows them instead of under-reading your samples.

How every number here is worked out

Concentration from A280

Beer–Lambert. The extinction coefficient can be given three ways, and they are not interchangeable:

A 0.1% — absorbance of a 1 mg/mL (1 g/L) solution in a 1 cm cuvette. This is what Benchling and ProtParam report, and the default here. c (mg/mL) = A / A0.1%. No MW needed.

E 1% — absorbance of a 10 mg/mL (1% w/v) solution. Exactly ten times A 0.1%, so mixing them up gives a 10-fold error. c (mg/mL) = 10 × A / E1%.

Molar ε (M⁻¹cm⁻¹) — c (M) = A / ε, then c (mg/mL) = c (M) × MW. MW required.

Replicate readings are averaged (SD shown for n ≥ 2). Dilution factor multiplies the result. Path length: NanoDrop's Protein A280 mode already reports a 10 mm-equivalent number, so no correction is applied by default.

Yield

Two steps. total (mg) = concentration (mg/mL) × volume of your prep (mL). Then yield (mg/L) = total (mg) ÷ culture volume (L), assuming the whole culture went into that one purification. Eluting into a bigger volume does not change how much protein you have — it is the same material spread thinner, so multiplying back out gives the same total.

What counts as a good yield depends entirely on the host, so pick the expression host in Step 3 and the verdict adjusts. All figures in mg per litre of culture:

E. coli  <10 low · 10–100 typical · 100–500 high · 0.5–10 g/L fed-batch · >10 g/L impressive · >100 g/L impossible
Yeast (P. pastoris)  <10 low · 10–100 typical · 0.1–1 g/L high · 1–15 g/L fermenter · >15 g/L impressive · >150 g/L impossible
Mammalian (CHO/HEK)  <10 low · 10–100 typical transient · 0.1–1 g/L high · 1–10 g/L routine fed-batch · >10 g/L impressive · >50 g/L impossible
Plant cell (BY-2)  <10 low · 10–100 typical · 100–500 high · 0.5–1.2 g/L very high · >1.2 g/L impressive · >20 g/L impossible

Each ceiling is a real physical limit, not a round number. E. coli at >100 gDCW/L still holds only ~55 g/L of total cellular protein (it is 52–55% protein by dry weight), so a single product above ~100 g/L cannot exist. CHO reaches far lower biomass than microbes, hence its lower ceiling. For plant suspension culture the highest titre ever reported, for any protein in any line, is 1.1 g/L in the BY-2 clone "Hulk" — so anything above that is genuinely remarkable, and 20 g/L is impossible.

Protein sequence → MW and ε280

MW is the sum of average residue masses plus one water (the values ExPASy ProtParam uses). ε280 follows Pace et al. 1995: ε = 5500·nTrp + 1490·nTyr + 125·nCystine M⁻¹cm⁻¹. Only disulfide-bonded cysteine (cystine) absorbs, so pick the redox state that matches your buffer. A 0.1% = ε / MW.

Blank and fit

With a blank selected, its mean absorbance is subtracted from every standard and sample before fitting. The quadratic option fits A = a₂C² + a₁C + a₀ by least squares and solves for C on the rising part of the curve; it warns if the curve turns over inside your standards.

Standard curve

Ordinary least squares through your standards: A = slope × C + intercept, inverted as C = (A − intercept) / slope. R² below 0.99 is flagged, and a sample landing outside the concentration range you actually tested is flagged as an extrapolation. R² is a useful QC indicator, not proof by itself that a curve is valid; inspect standards, blanks, replicates and residual behaviour as appropriate for your assay.
