Reaction & design settings
Polymerase & primer binding
This is which enzyme mix you're using to copy your DNA. Different brands recommend slightly different temperatures and timings — picking the right one here auto-fills the rest of the settings correctly for you.
Every primer’s binding region is designed to land near this — keeps the whole set at one annealing temp.
"Tm" (melting temperature) is the temperature at which a primer detaches from the DNA it's stuck to. This is the temperature we're aiming for — it directly determines what temperature you'll run your PCR machine at.
Hard ceiling — candidates above this are avoided whenever a shorter/alternate one exists; if a fragment's own sequence can't stay under it even at the length floor, that primer is honestly flagged rather than silently allowed through.
A safety cap. If your target Tm is 51°C but a primer's own DNA sequence is naturally very GC-rich, it might not be possible to get below, say, 65°C no matter what. This setting says "never go above this number" — and if that's genuinely impossible, it tells you clearly instead of quietly giving you a bad primer.
Soft target window the designer aims into (Phanta: 56–60 °C). Not a hard cut — sequence composition still wins.
A "nice to have" temperature range. The tool tries to land primers in this window when it can, but won't sacrifice a good match between your forward and reverse primer just to squeeze into this range.
On by default for Phanta Flash. Uncheck to use the vendor's Tm-based rule instead.
Standard range 18–30 nt, 20 nt ideal — length is a soft preference; Tm match between F/R always wins when the two clash, even if that means going outside this window.
"nt" = nucleotides = how many DNA letters (A/C/G/T) long the primer is. Most primers are 18–30 letters long. If making a primer slightly longer or shorter is what it takes to hit the right Tm, the tool will do that — getting the temperature right matters more than a perfect textbook length.
Overlap (homology arm)
Hard minimum 20 bp total (NEBuilder/SnapGene guidance) — will search longer automatically if a repeat is in the way.
In Gibson assembly, two DNA fragments join because they share a stretch of identical sequence at their ends — like puzzle pieces with matching edges. This setting is how long that shared/matching stretch needs to be (measured in base pairs, "bp") for the join to work reliably.
Each primer's own tail must be at least this long — checked FIRST, before homology Tm. The overlap is always split so no primer gets a near-zero overhang.
The shared "puzzle-piece" sequence above gets split between two primers — part of it is added onto one primer, part onto the other. This setting makes sure neither primer ends up with almost none of that shared sequence, which would make the join unreliable.
48–50 °C clears the 50 °C reaction. Estimated via the Wallace rule (2×(A+T)+4×(G+C)) — checked after the 15 bp/primer length floor is met.
The shared "puzzle-piece" sequence also needs its own melting temperature high enough to actually stick together during the assembly reaction (which runs at 50°C). This is that minimum.
>60 nt → PAGE/HPLC.
Conditions & homology screen
Used for BOTH the binding region and the overlap arm: flags a site with ≤ this many mismatches (≤1 in the terminal 5 nt) as a real off-target risk, not just exact repeats.
A primer should only stick to one place in your DNA. This checks for other spots where it might ALSO stick (even imperfectly) and cause the wrong product to be made. Higher number = stricter checking.
Fold parts/fragments shorter than this.
If you paste in a very short piece of DNA (like a small linker sequence), it's often not practical to PCR it separately — this setting decides how short is "too short to bother," and folds it into the neighboring primer's tail instead.
Primer3 default: flag AAAAAA-style runs longer than this.
A run of the same letter repeated many times (like AAAAAA) can make a primer bind poorly or slip during copying. This flags primers containing a run longer than this.
Design-standard 40–60% (Primer3Plus's hard limits are 20–80%; 40–60 is the preferred window)
GC% = what fraction of the primer is G or C letters (vs A or T). G/C pairs bind more strongly than A/T pairs, so a primer with too little or too much GC can behave unpredictably. 40-60% is the sweet spot most labs aim for.
Auto-set from the polymerase manual (Phanta Flash: ≤1 °C). Pairs are optimised to stay within this; flagged if they can't.
"F/R" = the Forward and Reverse primer for the same DNA piece. They both need to stick and let go at close to the same temperature, or one will work while the other doesn't. This is how close their temperatures need to be to each other.
How this tool designs

Expert Gibson practice, built in

📏
≥20 bp overlaps, GC-favoured. 20 bp is the accepted floor at the 50 °C reaction temperature (NEBuilder/SnapGene); the tool never goes below it, and prefers higher-GC arms when several options tie.
🧩
Whole-fragment overlaps first. Where possible the entire overlap is drawn from one flanking fragment's own tail/head — simplest, fewest primers modified — falling back to a split only if that's not unique.
🔎
Both binding region and overlap are homology-screened against every template and fragment, with mismatch tolerance (near-repeats count, not just exact ones) — the Primer-BLAST principle applied to Gibson design.
🌡️
Binding Tm matched F/R, 3′ GC clamp checked. Binding and overlap Tm are tracked separately — only the binding region matters for PCR annealing temperature.
Also worth doing at the bench: gel-purify PCR products before assembly if any non-specific bands appear; keep template plasmid (not PCR product) proportion low if linearizing by PCR; use a 1–2:1 insert:vector molar ratio as a starting point.
