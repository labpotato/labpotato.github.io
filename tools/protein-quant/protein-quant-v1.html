Help — how these numbers are calculated

Method 1 — A280 (MW & ε)

Beer–Lambert law. ε can be entered three ways, selected per row:

Molar (M⁻¹cm⁻¹): c (M) = A₁cm / ε, then c (mg/mL) = c (M) × MW. MW is required.

A 0.1%, i.e. the absorbance of a 1 mg/mL (1 g/L) solution at 1 cm: c (mg/mL) = A₁cm / A⁰·¹% directly, no MW needed.

E 1%, i.e. the absorbance of a 1% w/v, 10 mg/mL (10 g/L) solution at 1 cm: c (mg/mL) = 10 × A₁cm / E¹%, also no MW needed. This is a different number from A⁰·¹% by exactly a factor of 10 (E¹% = 10 × A⁰·¹%) — check which one a datasheet or export (e.g. Benchling) is actually reporting before entering it, since the two are easy to confuse and give a 10-fold error if swapped. MW is only needed, for either mass-based form, to also report a molar unit: c (M) = c (mg/mL) / MW.

Replicate A280 readings (comma/space/newline separated) are averaged, with SD shown for n≥2. Path length: almost every instrument's software, including the dedicated Protein A280 module on every NanoDrop model, already normalizes the displayed absorbance to a 10 mm-equivalent value before showing it to you — regardless of the tiny physical path (1 mm, auto-ranging to 0.05 mm) actually used internally. So the number you read off the screen, or pull from Benchling, is already what a standard cuvette would show, and needs no further correction: that is the default here. Only switch to a raw/custom path if you genuinely have a non-normalized reading — for example a manual bench spectrophotometer with a shorter cuvette that does not auto-correct, or NanoDrop's general-purpose UV-Vis module (as opposed to its Protein A280 module), which normalizes to 1 mm instead of 10 mm. A dilution factor multiplies the final result.

Method 2 — Standard curve

A set of known-concentration standards (classically BSA) is read alongside a colorimetric reagent (Bradford, BCA, etc.) and fit by ordinary least-squares linear regression: A = slope × C + intercept. Solving for concentration: C = (A − intercept) / slope. Each sample's replicate absorbance is averaged, converted through this equation, then multiplied by its dilution factor. If a sample's raw concentration falls outside the tested standard range, that is an extrapolation and is flagged. R² below 0.99 is flagged as below the linearity benchmark used in published assay-validation work.

Reaction volume

Solves C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ for V₁: V₁ = (C₂ × V₂) / C₁, where C₁ is the selected stock's concentration (from either method), C₂ is the target, and V₂ is the final volume. The remainder, V₂ − V₁, is buffer or other reaction components. A molar target requires a molecular weight for that stock; standard-curve samples only have one if you entered it in the optional MW column.

Yield

Total amount: mass (mg) = concentration (mg/mL) × total volume (mL), and amount (mol) = concentration (M) × total volume (L) when a molecular weight is available. If a starting amount is entered, % recovery is (yield / starting amount) × 100, computed in matching units (mass-to-mass or mole-to-mole). A result over 100% is flagged, since purification cannot increase total protein — it usually means a unit or starting-amount mismatch.

Protein per volume (e.g. mg protein per L culture): enter a separate starting/reference volume, and the total yield is divided by it: rate (mg/L) = total yield (mg) / reference volume (L). This is independent of the sample's own "total volume" field above — the reference volume is whatever you want the yield expressed against (typically the culture or lysate volume the prep started from), not the final eluate volume.
