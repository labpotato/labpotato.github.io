A single-file, offline plasmid editor for the browser, built to stand in for SnapGene on a machine that cannot spare the disk space.

**Files.** Opens SnapGene `.dna` and `.prot`, GenBank (`.gb .gbk .genbank .ape`), EMBL, FASTA (DNA or protein), plain text, Serial Cloner / DNA Strider `.xdna`, and its own `.json`. Saves SnapGene `.dna` (when the sequence came from a `.dna` file every packet of the original is kept and only the sequence, features, primers and notes are replaced, so annotations made here land in the same file SnapGene opens), GenBank with `/label` and colours, FASTA, and the map as SVG or PNG.

**Views.** Circular and linear maps with features, enzyme sites and primers, a sequence view with both strands, translations, enzyme sites and feature bars, and a Split view with map and sequence side by side and the selection synced. Drag to select on either; Find (DNA with IUPAC and mismatches, protein motifs in six frames, feature and enzyme names), Go to, keyboard navigation.

**Annotation and editing.** Features and primers with a dialog each; the selection becomes a feature (F) or a primer (P). Detect common features recognises standard backbone parts (origins, resistance genes, lacI, lacZα, CMV, T7, SP6, EGFP, polyA signals and more, taken from Addgene's annotated pUC19, pET-28b and pcDNA3-EGFP records) and tags and protease sites by translation. Typing, deleting, inserting and replacing sequence moves features along; undo and redo.

**Enzymes and digests.** About 230 common enzymes with sites and cut positions; sets for unique cutters, up to two cutters, all, or a chosen list, plus custom enzymes. The Digest tab lists fragments for any combination, draws a virtual gel, and opens a fragment as a new sequence.

**Primers and protein.** Nearest-neighbour Tm (SantaLucia 1998, salt-corrected), primer design for a selected amplicon with optional tails and restriction sites, binding-site search with 5′ tails tolerated. The Protein tab translates a selection, a CDS or a pasted protein and reports length, MW, pI, ε280 (Pace 1995), A 0.1%, GRAVY, aliphatic index, composition and a hydropathy plot, with a Send to AlphaFold 3 button that writes the AlphaFold Server job JSON and opens the site.

**v2.** One arrow grammar on the map (equal-thickness bands, head says direction, darker outline), translation tracks on the map with codon ticks and residue letters, primers drawn as thick half-arrows carrying their own bases (in the sequence view a forward primer sits above the top strand, a reverse one below the bottom strand written 3′→5′, mismatches red, tails dashed), a sequence ruler with hairlines and a hover position bubble, enzyme marks showing both cuts. Annotations panel grouped by kind with visibility boxes, click/Shift/Ctrl multi-select, a right-click menu, bulk show/hide/colour/type/translate/merge/delete, and drag to reorder lanes. Every sequence edit previews its effect on annotations and asks twice; typing is collected and confirmed on pause. Digest ends drawn with overhangs, compatible enzymes per end, a ligation check and a product builder. A find-anything box (position, DNA, protein motif in six frames, names, enzymes). Figure export for papers (Okabe–Ito, greyscale, outline; journal widths; SVG, PNG 300 dpi, PDF, copy). Codon optimisation to a host's preferred codons, pairwise alignment, ESMFold folding in the page with a pLDDT-coloured viewer, AlphaFold DB lookup, and a step-by-step AlphaFold 3 hand-off (the server has no API and needs a sign-in). The support line in the footer.

**v3.** Two foldable wings: the annotation list on the left and a new inspector on the right that shows whatever is selected (feature, primer, or a plain stretch) with the actions that belong to it. Either wing folds with the chevron in its header, Ctrl+\ and Ctrl+Shift+\, or the edge tab. The annotation list drops the redundant type column so names are no longer clipped, and each coding feature carries its own aa button; the Tools menu translates every coding feature at once or removes every translation, so translation goes on and off one at a time or all together. Primers can be linked into pairs: Design makes a linked forward and reverse, any two primers can be linked or unlinked from the list, the inspector or the primer dialog, the pair sits together in the list under its product size, and the map draws the amplicon as a dashed arc labelled with its length. Pairs survive a round trip through the SnapGene file. Map labels are now kept inside the canvas, with the lowest-priority sites dropped and counted rather than drawn on top of each other, and the translation track draws one quiet band instead of per-codon ticks when a codon is under a pixel. The toolbar no longer wraps and shows the sequence-lane options only on the sequence views, and the footer is a single line.

**v4.** SBOL Visual symbols for every part (bent arrow for a promoter, dome for a ribosome binding site, pointed box for a coding sequence, T for a terminator, circle for an origin, and the rest), drawn outside the band on the map and inside the bar in the sequence view, switchable under Symbols. Feature colours are grouped by what a part does, pinks for coding, blues for control, red and orange for stops, yellows for origins, teals for binding, greens for RNA, and band thickness follows the same idea. Copy now asks what you want: the bases, the reverse complement, the amino acids, the amino acids of the reverse complement, or FASTA, with Ctrl+Shift+C for the bases alone. Translation is reachable three ways that all agree, the aa button on a row, Shift+T on the selection, and the Translation group in Tools for all at once, and turning one on re-enables the translation lane if it was off. Primer names sit at the end of the arrow on the same line, forward and reverse alike. A sequence, feature or translation can be sent to NCBI BLAST. Edit and Tools are grouped under headings, figures default to the 1.5-column width, rows in the sequence view are separated with the strand pair and translation lanes on their own blocks, and the type is monospace with tabular figures.

**v5.** A **circuit view**, a third way of looking at a construct next to the map and the sequence. It drops the coordinates and draws what the plasmid does: each transcription unit on its own line, read 5′→3′ along its own strand, every part as its standard SBOL Visual symbol on a backbone line, and the regulatory links routed overhead the way a circuit diagram routes wires, a bar for repression and an arrowhead for activation. Schematic mode gives every part the same width because the order is the point; To scale places each part at its real coordinate, nudging a glyph clear of its neighbour with a grey leader back to the true position, and dropping a label to a second line rather than drawing it over the one before. The links are read off the part names and only in the prefix form (pTet is taken to be controlled by TetR, pLac by LacI), because "AmpR promoter" means the promoter that drives AmpR, not one that AmpR represses; they are a reading of the labels and never of the sequence, and they switch off.

Under the diagram every unit is **checked along the path the polymerase and the ribosome take**: a promoter to transcribe, a ribosome binding site 5 to 9 bases from the start codon, a start codon in frame, no internal stop, a stop codon, and a terminator to stop the read-through. Each unit comes out complete, complete with a caveat, or will not work as drawn, and every problem says what to change. In an operon each coding sequence after the first is checked for its own ribosome binding site, because a shared one only translates the first. The checks can only see what is annotated and say so.

A **parts library** of 44 entries: the Anderson constitutive promoter collection with its measured relative strengths, pLac, pTet, T7 and SP6; the B0030–B0034 ribosome binding sites; the B0010–B0015 terminators; five origins with copy numbers and compatibility groups; six resistance markers with working concentrations. Sequences and strengths come from the iGEM Registry part pages (CC BY 4.0), checked one at a time; a strength compares inside a family and never across families. Each family is listed strongest first and shows the placement rule people get wrong. Insert at the caret, replace a selection, or swap the selected part for another of the same kind, and the part is annotated as it lands, so the circuit view works on it straight away. Eleven entries, four of the five origins and four of the six markers, are reference cards with no sequence, because none has been checked against a primary record here; they carry the facts you look a part up for and point at Addgene for the bases.

**Fixes this release.** Enzyme cut marks used to be placed by arithmetic that assumed a fixed lane height, so as soon as a primer was on screen every tick sat about ten pixels below the bases it cuts, and a stagger that crossed the end of a row was dropped silently; they are now placed from the real position of the rendered strands, the label is centred over its own cut with a leader down to it, and a clipped stagger is drawn dashed to the edge. Primer names are drawn behind the arrow head, and the lane packer reserves the width of the name so two primers never share a line if their labels would meet. Empty primer lanes are no longer emitted on every row, which was costing a blank 25 px band per unused lane. Translations carry residue numbers. Feature bands carry a gradient. The long dropdowns spell out what the chosen option does. And the typing-ghost overlay was styled on a bare `.ghost`, which also matched every `.btn.ghost` in the chrome and pulled the undo, redo and panel-toggle buttons out of the layout; it has been scoped to the sequence view and they are visible again.

**Outside tools.** IDT's codon optimiser, the Salis Lab RBS Calculator, Clustal Omega at EMBL-EBI and CRISPRscan, under Tools → Other outside tools. None takes a sequence in the address, so each copies the right text and opens the page; the paste is the step that transmits anything. BLAST keeps its own entry and still fills the query box.

**v6.** **Ribosome binding strength**, calculated in the page. The thermodynamic model of Salis, Mirsky and Voigt (Nat Biotechnol 2009;27:946-50, doi:10.1038/nbt.1568) reimplemented from the model as published and as released by its authors: dG_total = dG_mRNA:rRNA + dG_spacing + dG_start - dG_mRNA - dG_standby, rate = 2500 exp(-dG/2.222), with their constants throughout - ATG -1.194, GTG -0.0748, TTG -0.0435 kcal/mol, an optimal aligned spacing of 5 nt, the anti-Shine-Dalgarno as the last nine bases of the E. coli 16S rRNA, a 35 nt fold window and a 4 nt standby site. Every term is shown, the Shine-Dalgarno is marked in the leader, and where the spacing is the limit it says how many bases to trim and what that is worth. It will also scan every start codon in the molecule, which is how an unannotated internal start shows itself.

The published implementation gets every folding energy from NUPACK. There is no NUPACK in a browser tab, so the folding is a Zuker minimum-free-energy fold over nearest-neighbour Turner parameters written for this, and the 16S binding sites are enumerated as gapless duplexes rather than by a full co-fold. The model is theirs, the folding is ours, and the result is an estimate on their scale rather than their number. Against the Registry sites, each in the same context, Spearman rho is 0.80 on four points, which is not significant: B0032 and B0031 land almost exactly, B0034 and B0030 swap, and the predicted spread is about 7x against a measured 14x. The methods note in the tool shows that table rather than claiming the model is fine.

**No promoter strength is reported**, on purpose. The tool finds the -35 and -10, measures the spacer and says which element is weak, and stops. The published model here is the 346-parameter Promoter Calculator of LaFleur, Hossain and Salis (Nat Commun 2022;13:5159, doi:10.1038/s41467-022-32829-5), fit to more than seventeen thousand measured promoters, which cannot be rebuilt from a description. A motif score of the kind that can be written by hand reaches Spearman rho 0.70 across the Anderson collection but ranks J23100, the reference of that family, twelfth, so the magnitude is withheld.

**Colours by measurement.** Hue carries the functional group and nothing else; lightness separates the types inside a group. The eight group hues were chosen by simulating deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia and measuring the CIE76 distance between every pair: the closest two stay 17.2 apart under red-green deficiency and 16.7 including tritanopia, against 0.9 for Okabe-Ito under tritanopia and 2.7 for a hand-picked saturated set. No ninth hue survives that test, so unclassified features take a recessive near-white instead of pretending to be a ninth category. Ink is chosen by contrast rather than a fixed luminance threshold, so every band clears 5.7:1.

**Interaction.** Both side panels drag to resize and double-click to reset, which is what the clipped names in the annotation list needed. The lane toggles moved out of the toolbar onto an icon rail beside the view with E, R, B, A and O to match, and the parts library, expression panel and figure export sit under them. The inspector renames, recolours and swaps a promoter, ribosome binding site, terminator or origin for another from the library, rewriting the bases in place. Clicking a residue in a translation selects its codon; Alt and a click hides a feature wherever it is drawn.

**A left click that does the work.** Selecting anything raises an action bar over the selection carrying the six things you actually do with a stretch of DNA: make a feature, make a primer, copy, translate, find matches, BLAST. A feature can be dragged by either end to change where it starts or stops, or Ctrl-dragged by the body to move it whole, with the position shown live and nothing committed until release, so one undo reverses the drag. Clicking an enzyme name opens a card with the cut position on both strands, the overhang, and buttons to select the site, digest with it, or show only that enzyme.

**Find matches, offline.** The selection, a feature or the whole sequence is searched against the other open sequences, the features of this one, and both part libraries, on both strands and then again as translated protein, by the seed-and-extend that BLAST itself is built on. Each hit is labelled by identity and by how much of the subject it covers, because an exact 60-base hit inside a 589-base origin is a fragment of that origin and not a match to it. The confident hits (95% or better over most of the part) can be annotated in one pass.

**BLAST, honestly.** NCBI does not send CORS headers on its URL API, so no page can read its results and nothing here pretends to. The search opens with the query filled in, and the results come back by paste: both the hit table (Download -> Hit Table) and the description table copied off the results page are parsed, each hit gets the same verdict wording as the local search, and hits carrying query coordinates become features.

**Fixed.** Swapping a part used to lose its annotation: replacing a feature's own bases deletes its whole span, which drops the feature, and both swap paths carried on editing an object that was no longer in the document. Present in v5 as shipped.

**v7.** **Readability.** Rows in the sequence view alternate white and light grey, carry a heavier rule and highlight as a whole on hover, so the eye holds one line across sixty bases. The faint greys were measured and raised: the bottom strand sat at 3.1:1 against the page, under the 4.5:1 floor for body text, and now reads 5.5:1; the small print went from 5.9:1 to 8.1:1.

**The action bar moved off the bases.** It used to appear over the selection that had just been made, covering it. It is docked to the right of the view as a column now, and the sequence reflows to leave room rather than running underneath it. (The reflow needed a fix in `measureChar`, which took `clientWidth` without subtracting padding, so reserving space had no effect on the column count.)

**Shift and click extends the selection anywhere**, including over a feature, a primer or an enzyme site. It had worked on bare sequence only: the feature, primer, ORF and enzyme handlers took the click first, and the drag handler added in v6 took it even earlier, in the capture phase.

**The circuit is drawn properly.** Parts are solid artwork rather than thin line symbols: filled bodies with a darker rim, a soft top highlight, rounded joins, on a heavier backbone. The shapes remain SBOL Visual, an open standard. The drawing idiom is the one figure tools use; none of their artwork is copied, because that artwork is proprietary.

**Autosearch over every reading frame.** Find matches will take every open reading frame above a chosen length, translate it, search it against everything in the file at DNA and protein level, and report. Frames matching a known part at 95% or better are offered for annotation in one pass; the rest carry a BLASTp button each, because only NCBI can place those and only the user should decide what the answer means. Nothing is annotated from a guess. BLASTp is also available for any selection, translated automatically, with a warning when the frame carries an internal stop.

**The circuit is drawn to the symbol table.** Heavy black line art for the promoter, ribosome binding site and terminator, colour only on the coding sequence: the gene is what changes between constructs while the regulatory shapes are constant, and a shape survives colour blindness and a photocopier where a hue does not.

**Parts change from the diagram.** Clicking a part in the circuit opens a card on it: rename, recolour, swap for another of the same kind from the library, check its strength, delete the annotation. Bases are rewritten in place.

**SEVA modules.** A SEVA vector is three interchangeable modules, each held between a fixed pair of unique cutters: antibiotic marker between SwaI and PshAI, origin between FseI and AscI, cargo between PacI and SpeI. Tools -> SEVA modules finds those six sites in whatever is open, reports which spans are swappable and which are not and why, and swaps one for the same module taken from another open plasmid or from pasted sequence. The full three-digit naming (10 markers, 19 origins, 57 cargo modules) is included for reading a SEVA number off a vector.

The module sequences are deliberately absent: seva-plasmids.com publishes only the first 25 bases of each and releases the rest through its own copy button, so there is nothing to reproduce honestly here.

*Fixed during this work:* the first version of the module swap replaced the span between the two cut positions. PacI cuts five bases into its own recognition site and SpeI one base into its, so that silently destroyed part of both flanking sites and the module could never be swapped again. The span is now the bases strictly between the two recognition sites, verified by swapping a cargo module and confirming the plasmid still scans as SEVA afterwards.

**The strength tools are where the parts are.** Selecting a ribosome binding site offers its strength in the inspector; a promoter offers the check; a coding sequence asks how well it is translated. Every transcription unit in the circuit view carries the same buttons. Previously this was a single line in a menu.

Everything runs in the page; nothing is uploaded except what you send to a folding service on purpose.
