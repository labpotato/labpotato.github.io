Colony Counter is a single-file, offline browser tool for counting bacterial colonies from a plate photograph and turning the count into CFU/mL. Open the HTML file in a browser — nothing to install, and the image never leaves your machine.

Load a photo (drop it, paste it, or pick a file), then press **Find the dish** and it locates the plate and counts. Loading an image on its own starts nothing. From there you correct anything it got wrong by clicking, and log the plate to a table that works out CFU/mL for you.

What it does:

- **Finds the dish automatically** — one click, no careful dragging round the rim. You can also draw a circle, a box, or a freehand lasso around an irregular area, or count the whole frame.
- **Corrects uneven lighting** before counting, with a morphological top-hat, so a plate lit from one side counts the same as one lit evenly.
- **Works out the colony appearance itself** — pale colonies on dark agar, dark colonies on pale agar, or chromogenic colonies that differ from the agar only in hue.
- **Separates clustered colonies in three passes** — a waist between them where there is one, the arcs their outlines still show when there isn't, and finally the leftover area for a colony buried in the middle of a cluster. Against clusters this takes the count error from 19% to 7% and recall from 72% to 93%, without inventing a single colony on an ordinary plate.
- **Estimates colonies inside solid clumps** that no distance-based method can cut apart, from their area, and flags them separately rather than folding them silently into the total.
- **Counts drop (spot) plates** — draw a circle round one spot, then a single click on each next spot places a circle the same size and counts it. Colonies you added or removed by hand belong to the spot they were made on and never follow you to the next one.
- **Ignores the agar's own grain** — nothing is counted unless it stands five noise standard deviations clear of the agar, measured on the same image. On a small, faint spot the old threshold landed inside the noise and counted every speck: on hand-counted spots from real drop plates it counted 421 where there were 17, and 636 on an empty footprint; it now counts 15 and 1. Where a spot is so densely packed with faint colonies that most fall just under that bar, it does not guess: the faint peaks are ringed in grey and reported next to the count ("⚠ 21 faint, not counted"), so you can raise the sensitivity or type the number yourself.
- **Rejects plate furniture** — the dish wall, condensation rings, scratches and marker strokes — before it can be counted or mistaken for a clump, and anything darker than the agar around it when the colonies are pale, which is what the lighter parts of a handwritten label are.
- **Colour-codes what it found** — green for confident, amber for anything worth a second look, vermillion for an estimated clump, black for marks you placed yourself. On deliberately hard test plates it flagged three of the detector's four remaining mistakes and none of its 675 correct finds.
- **Boosts low-resolution plates** — below about three pixels of colony radius it resamples onto a finer grid, which took ten touching pairs from 40 of 50 correctly resolved to 50 of 50. It adds no detail the camera did not record.
- **Recounts on its own** whenever anything that changes what's measured changes — brightness, contrast, gamma, local contrast, or the plate area — after you stop dragging. There's also a recount button that's always visible next to the colony count, so it never needs a trip back to the panel.
- **Lets you add or remove with one click** — while placing colonies by hand, clicking an existing mark removes it instead of adding a duplicate on top, so switching tools mid-correction is rarely needed. A dedicated Remove tool is still there for rapid-fire cleanup, and clicking picks the mark under the cursor correctly even when colonies sit close together.
- **Sensitivity scrolls** — in the colony menu under the count, nudge it with the mouse wheel and the count updates a moment after you stop. A one-click **Auto** button next to the image-adjustment controls sets brightness, contrast and gamma from the image itself.
- **Clear is always on the stage**, beside undo and redo, and on the Delete key.
- **Two independent size controls** — one scales the circles drawn on colonies the detector found, the other (on the stage, next to the Add tool) sets the size of the mark your own click leaves.
- **Keeps your manual corrections** when you change a setting and re-count, with undo and redo.
- **A hand-placed mark starts at roughly the size the detector is already finding**, not an arbitrary default, and resets to that whenever a new photo is loaded rather than carrying over a size dialled in for a different plate.
- **Reset to defaults** on the colony-settings menu restores automatic detection — sensitivity, splitting, clustering, any taught examples — in one click, without touching the plate area you already selected or the photo.
- **Teaching can be cleared mid-pick**, wiping the circles drawn so far without leaving picking mode, so a bad set of examples costs one click to redo rather than a full stop-and-restart.
- **Takes a count you typed** — the Colonies box follows the detected count until you type your own, or TNTC for a plate too numerous to count. It works with no photo loaded at all, for plates counted by eye. Counts can be corrected in the table afterwards.
- **Knows the plating method** — spread or pour plates are flagged outside 25–250 colonies (FDA BAM ch. 3), drop plates outside 3–30 per drop (Herigstad et al. 2001). Flagged, never enforced. An optional step moves the dilution on by one after each row, for a series counted spot by spot.
- **Rows carry a title that stays put** — typed once, it stays in the box for the next row, since consecutive rows are usually the same sample.
- **Summarises replicates** — mean CFU/mL with its SD, and mean ± SD of log₁₀ CFU/mL, the scale plate counts are usually compared on. With more than one title in the table, each sample is summarised on its own.
- **Exports** the table as a tab-separated .txt file — title, row, count, dilution, volume, CFU/mL, then the average, SD and log₁₀ summary, and a per-sample block — paste-ready into Excel as numbers, not as text carrying a unicode "×10ⁿ". **Counts only** gives just the colony numbers, one per line, as a file or straight to the clipboard. The marked-up image exports as a PNG carrying the count and the settings.
- **Remembers the table** in this browser, so a closed tab or a reload does not lose a morning's counting. It never leaves your machine.

Accuracy, on 17 synthetic plates with known colony counts: mean absolute count error **0.34%**, precision **100%**, recall **99.7%**. The harness that measures this ships alongside the tool in `validation/` and reads the detection core out of the published HTML, so you can re-run it yourself. On nine hand-counted circles from two real drop-plate photographs the count beyond the hand count's own uncertainty fell from 1,335 colonies in total to 47; four of the nine are within it, and the misses are glare from the dish wall, air bubbles and the edges of handwritten labels. **Keep spot circles clear of the dish wall and of labels**, and treat an automatic count as a first pass.

Everything runs in the page: no account, no upload, no server, no network request except the one-pixel analytics tag shared by the rest of the site.
