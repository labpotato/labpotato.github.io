Gibson Assembly Designer is an offline, single-file primer design tool for Gibson (isothermal) assembly. Paste in your fragments in the order they'll be assembled — circular or linear — and it designs Gibson-ready PCR primers using validated nearest-neighbour Tm calculations, holding every primer close to the target Tm and under the ceiling before anything else, and sizing each overlap to the field-standard 20 bp / ≥48 °C minimum.

**v8** checks every stretch of every primer — the overlap flap and the binding region alike, all lengths — against both strands of the source plasmid you attach, with an estimated Tm for each partial match (the primer's intended site is excluded; a second copy is not). Each primer gets a homology report: where the competing site is, how long the match is, whether its 3′ end can prime, and how it compares with the annealing temperature. When a primer has a problem it lists alternative primers that stay inside the Tm limits, best first, and swaps one in with a click.

**Step 3 layout.** Three levels. (1) *Your primers at a glance*: a verdict (how many primers are clear, worth a look, or need fixing), a list of what needs attention, one status tile per primer, and a large plasmid map with forward primers on the outer ring and reverse primers on the inner ring. Problem primers are red (amber for "worth a look") on the map, tiles, table and cards; clicking a primer anywhere opens its sequence map. (2) *All primers*: one table with editable sequences, Tm, Δ to the partner primer and the problems in plain words; edits recalculate in place and show what the sequence was. (3) *Each primer*: collapsible cards (sequence, properties, location, checks, sequence map, alternatives, homology, junction options), with a sticky jump bar and Expand all / Needs attention filters.

**Flap option (Step 3).** Above the table, *Standard flaps* or *Short or no flaps*. With a source plasmid attached, short or no flaps are used where the template really carries the overlap. With **no plasmid attached** and the fragments directly adjacent, the source is *assumed* to continue into the neighbour; every affected primer, the table and the notes say **assumed, not verified**. Where sequence is inserted between fragments, nothing is assumed.

The **junction options** cover the fixes for high homology: move or resize the overlap, add a unique spacer for a non-coding junction (screened for restriction sites, ATG, Shine-Dalgarno-like sequence, repeats and similarity to your sequences), or — when the source plasmid of one fragment already carries the neighbour's sequence — build the primer pair with short or no overlap flaps by extending the PCR along the template. The two PCR products are rebuilt from the primers and checked to overlap by at least the minimum, in every mode.

The primer navigator shows every primer as a chip: the one being described is solid and the rest are shaded, and the same primer is bright on the alignment and the map while its neighbours are grey. Results include an order sheet, an illustrative map, and "Why?" toggles explaining each primer's length and Tm.

It runs entirely in the browser (no sequence data leaves the page) and needs no installation — just open the HTML file.

**Changing the flap option visibly changes the primers.** After *Short or no flaps* or *Standard flaps*, the page lists every primer that changed (length, flap, binding region, Tm) and marks it in the table. When nothing can change, it says which junction keeps its flaps and why (template does not continue into the neighbour, or sequence is inserted between the fragments) and offers *I know my source carries the overlap: make them flap-free anyway*, which is marked as not verified. Primers you edited by hand keep your sequence, and the page says so.

**Step 3, compact version.** One table (status, primer, editable sequence, length, Tm, a *flapless* tick per junction and a tick for all, problems) with the required Gibson overlap and each junction's measured overlap above it. Each primer card opens to its sequence map: **drag across the construct** to try a different primer and see its binding length, Tm, flap, and the overlap the products would then have, then press *Use this sequence*. The map header shows the overlap now, overlap Tm and primer Tm. Properties and location, checks, alternatives, homology and junction options are dropdowns.
