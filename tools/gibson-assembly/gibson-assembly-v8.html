Gibson Assembly Designer

Primers with matched Tm, overlaps that clear the 50 °C reaction, and every binding site checked against the whole plasmid you are actually amplifying from — not just the piece you are keeping. The map on the right redraws as you type. Nothing leaves your browser.

Working
Step 1

Parts, in assembly order

🧬
Enter each part 5′→3′, in the order it appears in the finished construct. A part shorter than the fold-in threshold (default 50 bp) isn't PCR'd on its own — it gets built into the neighbouring primer's tail instead. Attach the source plasmid to any part you PCR out of a larger plasmid: that is what lets the off-target check see the whole template, not just your insert.

Templates you'll PCR from

plasmids on your shelf, gBlocks, gDNA…
🔍
Paste every plasmid that might contribute a piece. Each region of your target gets traced back to the template it came from, on either strand, wrapping the origin if needed — then the primers for that region are screened against that entire template, so a primer that would also stick somewhere else in the same plasmid is caught before you order it.
Step 2

Which enzyme and kit are you using?

🧬
Sometimes the overlap between two fragments already exists in the template: the plasmid one fragment comes from continues into its neighbour. Then the PCR only needs to reach a little further along the template, and the primers need no overlap flap at all. This is checked against both templates, and the products are verified to overlap by at least the minimum overlap length. It needs the source plasmid attached to each part. Where the template does not carry the sequence, ordinary flaps are used.
